## Step 2 — Project at a Glance

### What problem it solves

InsureIQ is a **multi-tenant SaaS platform for insurance companies** that also operates a **consumer-facing insurance comparison marketplace**. Two interlinked problems:

1. **For insurers (B2B):** full policy lifecycle management — underwriting, claims, agent CRM, admin tooling — on a white-labeled, configurable platform rather than bespoke software.
2. **For insurance consumers (B2C):** compare policies across multiple insurers in one place, complete KYC once, and track all policies and claims in a single dashboard regardless of insurer.

### Core value proposition

InsureIQ gives insurance companies a ready-made operational platform with a built-in distribution channel (the B2C marketplace), reducing both IT cost and customer acquisition cost. Consumers get a single trusted place to compare, buy, and manage insurance across every provider — modeled on Policybazaar, Compare the Market, and Insurify, extended with an AI intelligence layer in Phase 2.

### Domain terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tenant | An insurance company subscribing to InsureIQ's platform |
| Policy | An insurance contract issued to a customer |
| Underwriting | Evaluating risk and deciding whether to issue a policy and at what premium |
| Premium | The periodic payment a customer makes to hold a policy |
| Claim | A policyholder's request for financial compensation |
| Claims Adjuster | Employee who reviews, verifies, and settles claims |
| Commission | A percentage of premium paid to the selling agent |
| KYC | Know Your Customer — identity verification required before policy issuance |
| Universal Risk Score | A portable, customer-owned cross-insurer risk indicator (Phase 2) |
| Marketplace Lead | Record created when a B2C quote request is routed to an insurer tenant |
| Churn / Retention | Probability that a customer lets their policy lapse without renewal |
| SLA | Service Level Agreement — time target for settling claims |
| Federated Learning | AI approach where model weights (not raw data) are shared across tenants |
| SHAP | Explainability technique showing which features drove an AI decision |
| RLS | PostgreSQL Row-Level Security — DB-layer tenant data isolation |
| CDC | Change Data Capture — streaming DB changes into an analytics warehouse |

## Step 3 — User Types & Actors

### 3.1 End-Customer (Tenant Policyholder)

**Who:** An individual who bought insurance from one specific insurer and uses that insurer's white-labeled portal. They never see the InsureIQ brand — the portal is fully branded to the insurer (e.g., `app.abcinsurance.com`). Low-frequency user.

**Goals:** Apply for policies, track applications, file and monitor claims, renew policies, manage identity documents.

**Permissions:**
- ✅ Create: applications, claims, account profile
- ✅ View: own policies, own claim status (no internal adjuster notes), own documents
- ❌ Cannot see: other customers' data, insurer's internal configuration, pricing logic

**Entry point:** Self-signup at `{tenant}.insureiq.app` → 3-step KYC (identity details → document upload → verification pending)

**Key workflows:**
1. Sign up → KYC → Verified → Home
2. Home → Browse policy types → Application form → Upload documents → Submit
3. Track application status (Submitted → Under review → Approved/Rejected)
4. Policy detail → File a claim → Upload incident photos/docs → Submit
5. Monitor claim stepper (Submitted → Under review → Fraud check → Approved → Payment sent)
6. Renewal prompt → repeat application flow

**Dependencies:** Underwriter decides on application. Claims Adjuster processes claim. Tenant Admin configures available policy types.

**UI screens:** Sign up/KYC, Home/My Policies, Browse & Apply, Application Status, File a Claim, Claim Tracker, Policy Detail, Profile & Documents.

**Mobile:** Heavy mobile usage especially for claims. Bottom tab bar. Auto-saved drafts.

---

### 3.2 Agent

**Who:** A sales and relationship manager employed by the insurer tenant. Manages a portfolio of assigned customers, prevents churn, submits applications on customers' behalf.

**Goals:** Know which customers need attention now, submit applications on behalf of customers, track commissions, retain at-risk customers.

**Permissions:**
- ✅ View: own assigned customers only
- ✅ Create: applications on behalf of customers
- ✅ Edit: outreach logs on retention alerts
- ❌ Cannot see: in-progress underwriting decisions, adjuster workspace, policy configuration, other agents' customers

**Entry point:** Credential login at `{tenant}.insureiq.app`; account created by Tenant Admin with Agent role.

**Key workflows:**
1. Dashboard → Review renewal pipeline and retention alerts
2. Customer detail → Submit application on their behalf → Monitor to approval
3. Retention alert → Log outreach → Alert cleared to history
4. Commission tracker → Filter by date → Export CSV

**Dependencies:** Tenant Admin creates account and assigns customers. Underwriter processes submitted applications. Churn-prediction AI (Phase 2) populates retention worklist.

**UI screens:** Agent Dashboard, Customer Portfolio, Customer Detail, New Application (on behalf of), Retention Alerts, Commission Tracker.

**Mobile:** Field-friendly — customer detail and "log outreach" are priority one-tap actions.

---

### 3.3 Underwriter

**Who:** A specialist who reviews applications, assesses risk, and decides to approve/reject/refer. Makes 30–80 decisions per shift. Highest-frequency, highest-stakes B2B role.

**Goals:** Work through queue efficiently, make defensible auditable decisions, set premium within bounds, review decision accuracy over time.

**Permissions:**
- ✅ View: all pending applications in their tenant
- ✅ Action: approve, reject, or refer any application
- ✅ Adjust: recommended premium within tenant-configured bounds
- ❌ Cannot change: policy-type configuration or premium bands (Admin's territory)

**Entry point:** Credential login at `{tenant}.insureiq.app`; account created by Tenant Admin.

**Key workflows:**
1. Queue dashboard → sort by risk score → open highest-priority application
2. Review applicant data, documents, risk breakdown (Phase 2 SHAP)
3. Adjust premium if needed → Approve / Reject / Refer with mandatory justification note
4. Return to queue; repeat
5. Periodically check decision history and model performance

**Dependencies:** End-Customer or Agent submits the application. Risk-scoring AI (Phase 2) augments data. Tenant Admin sets the premium-band bounds.

**UI screens:** Underwriting Dashboard (Queue), Application Detail, Decision History, Model Performance (Phase 2 only).

**Mobile:** Desktop-primary. Lightweight mobile for queue status and routine approvals only.

---

### 3.4 Claims Adjuster

**Who:** Employee who reviews, verifies, and settles claims. Works in two speeds — fast-lane (routine) and slow-lane (fraud-flagged/complex investigations).

**Goals:** Process routine claims quickly, detect fraud, maintain SLA compliance, produce auditable decisions.

**Permissions:**
- ✅ View: all claims in their tenant; full claimant policy and claim history
- ✅ Action: approve, reject, or escalate any claim
- ✅ Edit: OCR-extracted data fields (Phase 2); corrections feed retraining
- ❌ Cannot see: cross-tenant raw claim data (only hashed fraud signals in Phase 2 Part B)

**Entry point:** Credential login at `{tenant}.insureiq.app`; account created by Tenant Admin.

**Key workflows:**
1. Claims dashboard → queue sorted by fraud confidence → open highest-risk claim
2. View source documents alongside extracted data → verify/correct fields
3. Review fraud-flag explanation (Phase 2 SHAP) → approve / reject / escalate
4. Escalated → Investigation view → full claimant history + related signals → multi-day notes thread → resolve
5. End of day → SLA tracker → catch up aging claims

**Dependencies:** Customer submits the claim. Fraud-detection AI (Phase 2) scores it. OCR/NLP (Phase 2) extracts structured data. Tenant Admin configures SLA target and fraud-confidence threshold.

**UI screens:** Claims Dashboard, Claim Detail, Investigation View, SLA Tracker.

**Mobile:** Desktop-primary — document review needs real screen space.

---

### 3.5 Tenant Administrator

**Who:** The operations/IT manager of a single insurer tenant. Configures the platform, manages users, monitors KPIs, controls billing. Hub-and-spoke role — no single repeated daily task.

**Goals:** Right people in right roles, configure underwriting rules, monitor business health, manage billing, audit past decisions.

**Permissions:**
- ✅ Create/edit/suspend: any user within own tenant
- ✅ Configure: policy types, coverage rules, premium bands, approval thresholds (tenant-scoped only)
- ✅ View: full tenant audit log
- ✅ Enable/disable: AI features within plan tier
- ❌ Cannot see: any other tenant's data or configuration
- ❌ Cannot provision new tenants (Super-Admin only)

**Entry point:** First account provisioned by Super-Admin during tenant onboarding. Subsequent admins invited via Team page.

**Key workflows:**
1. Onboarding: receive account → configure policy types → invite team
2. Daily dashboard glance → KPI cards + activity feed
3. New hire: Team → Invite → Assign role
4. Quarterly: Policy settings → Adjust premium bands → Versioned save
5. Monthly: Billing → Review usage → Download invoice
6. Reactive: Dispute arises → Audit log → Filter to decision → Verify

**Dependencies:** Super-Admin provisions their tenant and creates their initial account.

**UI screens:** Admin Dashboard, Team (User Management), Policy Settings, AI Settings (Phase 2), Billing & Invoices, Audit Log.

**Mobile:** Dashboard glance mobile-friendly; everything else desktop.

---

### 3.6 InsureIQ Super-Admin (Platform Operator)

**Who:** An InsureIQ employee (not any tenant's staff) managing the platform itself. Operates in a separate, visually distinct console (`console.insureiq.com`). Low-frequency, alert-driven.

**Goals:** Onboard new insurers, monitor platform health, oversee billing, manage AI models across tenants (Phase 2), investigate escalated issues.

**Permissions:**
- ✅ Create: new tenants
- ✅ View: all tenants' configuration, usage, billing; platform-wide audit log; model registry (Phase 2)
- ✅ Access: read-only view of any tenant's detail for investigation
- ❌ Cannot: directly access or modify tenant-specific underwriting/claims data

**Entry point:** Logs in at `console.insureiq.com` — completely separate application shell with distinct visual chrome (darker, more technical, no tenant branding).

**Key workflows:**
1. Provision new tenant: 4-step wizard (name/subdomain → plan & isolation mode → initial admin account → review & provision)
2. Daily: Platform overview → confirm alert panel is empty
3. Investigation: Search → find tenant → view configuration/usage/billing
4. Billing review: Aggregate MRR, per-tenant invoice status, commission ledger
5. Alert-driven: Model drift (Phase 2) → Model registry → Retrain or rollback

**Dependencies:** Root authority — no upstream dependencies. The tenant admin's world begins when Super-Admin provisions their tenant.

**UI screens:** Platform Overview, Tenant Directory, Tenant Provisioning Wizard, Billing Oversight, Model Registry (Phase 2), Platform Audit Log.

**Mobile:** Desktop-only by design.

---

### 3.7 Marketplace Customer (B2C)

**Who:** An individual on InsureIQ's own consumer site (`insureiq.com`) comparing insurance across providers. Same kind of human as the End-Customer but in a completely different context — no loyalty to InsureIQ or any specific insurer. Two distinct modes: first-time visitor (linear guided flow) vs. returning customer (dashboard-first).

**Goals:** Compare plans from multiple insurers without visiting each site individually, apply without re-entering data, track all policies/claims across every insurer in one dashboard, control data sharing (Phase 2).

**Permissions:**
- ✅ View: own cross-tenant policy and claim aggregation; public marketplace catalog
- ✅ Create: quote requests, applications (routed to a tenant), consent grants (Phase 2)
- ❌ Cannot see: other customers' data; tenant internal configuration or pricing logic

**Entry point:** Self-signup at `insureiq.com`; KYC completed once (Document Vault); identity reused across all future applications.

**Key workflows:**
1. First visit: Home → Select category → Quote form → Comparison results → Plan detail → Apply (routed) → Hand-off confirmation → My Insurance
2. Returning: land on My Insurance → check existing policies/claims
3. Adding coverage: My Insurance → Compare & Apply → same shopping flow
4. Consent management (Phase 2): Prompt during application → Consent center → Review or revoke

**Dependencies:** Applications processed by selected insurer's Underwriter. Claims processed by that insurer's Adjuster. Universal Risk Score (Phase 2) requires consent framework.

**UI screens:** Home/Landing, Quote Request Form, Comparison Results, Plan Detail, Application (Routed), My Insurance (Unified Dashboard), Unified Claim Tracker, Document Vault, AI Advisor (Phase 2), Universal Risk Score & Consent Center (Phase 2).

**Mobile:** Mobile-heavy. Single-column cards, camera-first document capture, bottom tab bar.

## Step 4 — Cross-User Interaction Map

### Flow A — New Policy (B2B Direct)

```
Customer submits application (Browse & Apply)
  → System: creates application record "pending"; increments usage meter
  → Underwriter: sees it in queue sorted by risk score
  → Underwriter: reviews, approves/rejects/refers with mandatory note
    → Approved: System emits policy.issued event
      → Customer: notified; Application Status shows Approved; Home shows new policy card
      → Agent (if agent-submitted): commission ledger updated
    → Rejected: Customer notified; Application Status shows rejection reason
```

### Flow B — New Policy (Marketplace → Tenant)

```
Marketplace Customer requests quote (Quote Request Form)
  → System: fans quote in parallel to every relevant tenant
  → Each tenant: returns quote (static bands Phase 1, risk model Phase 2)
  → System: creates MARKETPLACE_LEAD record per tenant response
  → Customer sees Comparison Results (trust-strip cards)
  → Customer selects an insurer → Application pre-filled from Document Vault
  → Application enters that tenant's underwriting queue (identical to Flow A from Underwriter's view)
  → Underwriter processes (same as Flow A)
  → policy.issued event → Unified Dashboard (B2C) updated → My Insurance shows new policy
```

### Flow C — Claim Filing & Processing

```
Customer files a claim (File a Claim form — mobile-first, auto-saves)
  → System: creates claim "submitted"; Customer sees Claim Tracker immediately
  → (Phase 2) OCR/NLP extracts structured data from uploaded documents
  → (Phase 2) Fraud model scores claim → confidence attached
  → Claims Adjuster: sees claim in queue sorted by fraud confidence
  → Low fraud confidence → Adjuster: verifies extracted data → approves/rejects with note
  → High fraud confidence → Adjuster: escalates → Investigation View
    → Multi-day notes thread; full claimant history; cross-tenant hashed signals (Phase 2 Part B)
    → Eventually: approve/reject from Investigation View itself
  → Customer notified → Claim Tracker reaches final state
  → (Phase 2 Part B) Hashed fraud pattern contributed to shared intelligence (never raw data)
```

### Flow D — Tenant Onboarding

```
New insurer decides to use InsureIQ
  → Super-Admin: 4-step provisioning wizard (name/subdomain → plan & isolation → initial admin → review)
  → System: provisions schema/DB, seeds default templates, allocates subdomain, creates billing subscription
  → Tenant Admin: receives credentials → configures policy types, premium bands, approval thresholds
  → Tenant Admin: invites underwriters, adjusters, agents
  → Tenant is live: End-Customers can sign up and apply
```

### Flow E — Agent Retention Cycle (Phase 2)

```
Churn model scores a policyholder as at-risk
  → System: creates retention alert on Agent's worklist; sidebar badge count increments
  → Agent: opens Customer Detail → reviews policy and risk situation
  → Agent: contacts customer externally
  → Agent: logs outreach (one-click in system)
  → Alert: moves to resolved history (never deleted — contact record retained for audit)
```

### Flow F — Consent Grant (Phase 2 Part B)

```
Marketplace Customer applies for a policy with Insurer B
  → Insurer B: requests access to Universal Risk Score
  → System: surfaces consent prompt during application
  → Customer: reviews scope (data elements, duration, counterpart) → grants or declines
  → If granted: CONSENT_GRANT record created
  → Universal Risk Score pipeline: reads grant before accessing any cross-tenant data
  → Customer: can view and revoke any grant from Consent Center at any time
```

## Step 6 — Data Model Understanding

### Core Entities (from SDD ER diagram)

| Entity | Description | Key Attributes | Relationships |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TENANTS** | Insurance company subscribed to the platform | id, name, subdomain, isolation_mode, plan_tier | Has many USERS, POLICY_TYPES, POLICIES, CLAIMS |
| **USERS** | Insurer tenant employees (agents, underwriters, adjusters, admins) | id, tenant_id, role, email | Belongs to one TENANT |
| **CUSTOMERS** | Platform-level entity — one per real person across all tenants | id, kyc_status, universal_id | Has many POLICIES, CLAIMS, DOCUMENTS, MARKETPLACE_LEADS, CONSENT_GRANTS |
| **POLICY_TYPES** | Category of insurance product defined by a tenant | id, tenant_id, coverage_rules, premium_bands, approval_thresholds | Belongs to TENANT; POLICIES are instances |
| **POLICIES** | Active insurance contract between a customer and a tenant | id, tenant_id, customer_id, policy_type_id, status, premium | Belongs to TENANT + CUSTOMER; has CLAIMS + RISK_SCORES |
| **CLAIMS** | Request for compensation filed against a policy | id, tenant_id, policy_id, status, amount_claimed | Belongs to TENANT + POLICY; has DOCUMENTS + FRAUD_FLAGS |
| **DOCUMENTS** | Uploaded files (KYC, claim evidence, policy PDFs) | id, customer_id or claim_id | Belongs to CUSTOMER and/or CLAIM |
| **RISK_SCORES** | AI-generated risk assessment for a policy/application | id, policy_id, model_version, score | Belongs to POLICY |
| **FRAUD_FLAGS** | AI-generated fraud assessment for a claim | id, claim_id, model_version, confidence | Belongs to CLAIM |
| **MARKETPLACE_LEADS** | Records a B2C quote request routed to a specific tenant | id, customer_id, routed_tenant_id, status | Belongs to CUSTOMER; routed to one TENANT |
| **CONSENT_GRANTS** | Explicit record of what data a customer authorized to share | id, customer_id, data_scope, granted_to, expiry | Belongs to CUSTOMER |

### Critical Design Decision

`CUSTOMERS` is **platform-level** (not tenant-scoped), identified by `universal_id`. `POLICIES` and `CLAIMS` are tenant-scoped with `tenant_id`. This is the architectural decision that makes both "insurer data stays isolated" and "customer sees all their policies in one dashboard" simultaneously true — they no longer conflict.

## Step 7 — Non-Functional & Cross-Cutting Concerns

### Security & Auth

- **JWT-based auth** with `tenant_id` embedded; re-validated on every request
- **Tenant context propagated** to all layers: HTTP handlers, background jobs, cron tasks, webhook handlers (not just HTTP)
- **PostgreSQL RLS** enforced at DB layer — a forgotten WHERE clause fails closed, not open
- **Enterprise tenants** get schema-per or DB-per isolation
- **Super-Admin console** is structurally separate — a bug cannot accidentally expose a tenant admin to another tenant's console
- **Consent-gated data access** — `CONSENT_GRANTS` enforced at query layer for all cross-tenant AI (Phase 2)

### Performance & Scale

- Analytics reads go to a **separate OLAP warehouse** (BigQuery/ClickHouse) — never to tenant operational DBs
- **Event bus** decouples B2B operations from B2C read-model updates (no synchronous cross-boundary calls)
- **Modular monolith** — service-extractable when load profiles diverge (Phase 2+)
- Noisy-neighbor risk for shared-schema tenants acknowledged; dedicated-infrastructure tier is the mitigation and commercial upsell

### Compliance & Regulatory

- **Ranking transparency:** Marketplace ranking algorithm + sponsored placement must be disclosed in-product (UK FCA and equivalent regulatory standard cited)
- **Explainability by default:** Every AI-assisted decision logs model version + SHAP reasoning — "the model said so" is not audit-defensible
- **Consent framework:** Phase 2 Part B must be *technically incapable* of moving raw tenant data cross-boundary — safeguard in the pipeline, not just in policy docs
- **Shared Fraud Intelligence:** Hashed identifiers only; differential-privacy noise on federated learning updates

> **⚠️ Gap — Regulatory jurisdiction unclear.** Both India (Policybazaar references → IRDAI) and UK (FCA references) are mentioned. The actual jurisdiction determines specific compliance obligations (data retention, consent mechanics, audit requirements).

## Step 9 — Suggested Build Order (Conceptual, Dependency-Based)

1. **Auth & Multi-Tenancy Foundation**
   JWT auth with tenant_id, tenant resolution middleware, RLS/schema isolation, Super-Admin console, tenant provisioning wizard. Nothing else can work without verified tenant context.

2. **Tenant Administrator Core**
   User management (invite/role assignment) and Policy Type configuration (premium bands, coverage rules). Underwriters and adjusters cannot work without accounts and rules.

3. **End-Customer Identity (KYC)**
   Sign-up and KYC verification. Document storage must also exist before this step. No policy can be issued without a verified customer.

4. **Policy & Underwriting — B2B**
   Application intake (customer + agent paths), APPLICATIONS entity lifecycle, underwriting queue, decision workflow (approve/reject/refer with note), policy.issued event emission. The event bus must be running to fire this event.

5. **Agent CRM**
   Customer portfolio, customer detail, commission tracker. Depends on customers (Step 3) and policies (Step 4).

6. **Claims — B2B**
   Claim intake, claims queue, claim detail, SLA tracker, investigation view. Depends on policies (Step 4).

7. **Billing & Usage Metering**
   Usage counters (policies/claims per tenant), tenant invoicing, feature-tier gating at API level (HTTP 403 for AI endpoints on lower tiers). Must run alongside Step 4 since metering starts from day one.

8. **B2C — Marketplace Customer Identity & Document Vault**
   Marketplace customer sign-up, KYC (reusing component from Step 3), Document Vault. Must exist before quote/comparison flow.

9. **B2C — Quote, Comparison & Routing**
   Quote fan-out engine, comparison results with trust-strip + ranking + sponsored labeling + "How we rank" disclosure, plan detail, application routing to tenant queue (Step 4). MARKETPLACE_LEAD record creation.

10. **B2C — Unified Dashboard**
    Unified Policy & Claims Dashboard (read-model, event-bus-fed). Event bus must consume policy.issued and claim-status events from tenant cores.

11. **Notifications**
    Email/SMS/push delivery for all triggers identified in Step 7. Can run in parallel with any step above but realistically slots in after the core flows that generate triggers exist.

12. **Phase 2 Part A — Per-Tenant AI**
    Risk scoring (Underwriter), Fraud detection (Adjuster), Document Intelligence (Adjuster), Churn Prediction (Agent). Each plugs into an already-stable B2B workflow. Model registry and drift monitoring added to Super-Admin and Tenant Admin consoles.

13. **Phase 2 Part B — Cross-Tenant AI (in internal sequence)**
    AI Insurance Advisor (low governance, reuses existing comparison flow) → Universal Risk Score + Consent Framework → Shared Fraud Intelligence (hashed matching first, federated learning later). Consent framework is a hard prerequisite for Universal Risk Score.
